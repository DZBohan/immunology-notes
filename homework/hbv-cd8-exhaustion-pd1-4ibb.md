# Chronic HBV Infection: CD8⁺ T-Cell Dysfunction and Experimental Design

## Assignment requirements

### 1. Mechanistic explanation

A patient with chronic HBV infection has persistent viral antigen and detectable HBV-specific CD8⁺ T cells. However, these T cells show reduced cytokine production and cytotoxic activity. Using concepts discussed in this course, explain why the presence of antigen-specific CD8⁺ T cells does not necessarily result in effective viral clearance. Your answer should integrate TCR–pMHC recognition, the three-signal model of T-cell activation, co-stimulatory and co-inhibitory signaling, cytotoxic T-cell function, and T-cell exhaustion.

### 2. Experimental Design

Persistent HBV antigen may drive T-cell dysfunction through excessive inhibitory signaling and insufficient co-stimulation. Design an ex vivo experiment to test whether combined PD-1 blockade and 4-1BB stimulation restores HBV-specific CD8⁺ T-cell function more effectively than either treatment alone.

Your answer should include:

- A clear hypothesis
- Experimental samples/system
- Experimental and control groups
- At least three functional readouts
- Expected results
- At least one alternative outcome and interpretation
- Major limitations and potential clinical significance

A strong experimental design should distinguish between T-cell activation, proliferation, cytokine production, and cytotoxic function, rather than relying on a single readout.
